  1. Saving as a Luxury Good: Why Progressive Taxation Cannot Guarantee Falling Inequality
    Arashk Afshar
    2026
    Job Market Paper
    Abs

    When the rich save a systematically larger share of their wealth, the distribution pulls apart on its own, and no conventional tax instrument inherits the classical guarantee that progressivity compresses it: commodity taxes can reverse their own equalization, and income taxes fail for every luxury preference. The wealth tax alone reaches accumulated wealth at its source, yet it too carries no guarantee—what governs the long-run trajectory is not the tax rate but the elasticity of retention, how post-tax wealth scales with pre-tax wealth at the top. Because every enacted or proposed wealth tax is asymptotically proportional there, it leaves this elasticity essentially undisturbed, and the debate has been conducted over the wrong parameter.

  2. The Myth of the Informed Voter
    Arashk Afshar
    2026
    Abs

    This paper develops a model of political campaigns in which advertising manipulates voters not by informing them but by directing their attention to issues chosen by candidates, who are selected by elites. Under unlimited campaign funding, the equilibrium concentrates voter attention on a narrow set of issues and favors more extreme candidates. Restricting funding reverses this: the equilibrium shifts toward a more moderate candidate and a better-informed electorate, offering an equilibrium rationale for campaign-finance limits without abandoning voter rationality.

  3. Saving as Instrument and Status: Progressive Taxation in a Dynamic Economy
    Arashk Afshar
    2026
    Abs

    Extends the static analysis to a life-cycle economy, where saving carries additional force as both an instrument and a marker of status; preliminary work suggests this strengthens, rather than weakens, the case for marginal progressivity.
